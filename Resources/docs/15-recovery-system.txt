=====================================
1. PURPOSE
=====================================

This document defines the recovery system for FitOnus, covering recovery score calculation, rest day recommendations, active recovery routines, muscle group recovery tracking, and AI-driven recovery guidance. Recovery is essential for sustained fitness progress, and this system ensures users understand when to push harder and when to rest.

=====================================
2. OVERVIEW
=====================================

The recovery system synthesizes data from multiple sources — sleep quality, workout intensity, heart rate variability (HRV), training load, and subjective readiness — to produce a daily recovery score (0-100). This score guides training decisions: high scores suggest readiness for intense training, while low scores recommend lighter activity or rest. The system tracks per-muscle-group recovery to prevent overtraining.

=====================================
3. GOALS
=====================================

  - Provide a daily recovery score that guides training intensity decisions.
  - Track recovery per muscle group to prevent overtraining.
  - Recommend rest days and active recovery routines.
  - Integrate sleep, heart rate, and workout data into a holistic recovery assessment.
  - Enable the AI Coach to provide recovery-aware workout recommendations.

=====================================
4. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
=====================================

  - Daily recovery score (0-100) calculated from available data.
  - Recovery score breakdown showing contribution of each factor.
  - Muscle group recovery status (recovered, recovering, fatigued) based on last training date and intensity.
  - Active recovery routine suggestions (stretching, mobility, light cardio).
  - Recovery trend chart over time.
  - Training load analysis: acute vs. chronic load ratio.
  - Subjective readiness survey (optional daily check-in).
  - Rest day recommendations based on recovery score and training history.

=====================================
5. NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
=====================================

  - Recovery score must compute within 500ms.
  - Recovery dashboard must load within 1 second.
  - Muscle group recovery status must reflect the most recent workout data.
  - Recovery score must update automatically when new data arrives (sleep logged, workout completed).

=====================================
6. USER FLOW
=====================================

  6.1 Viewing Recovery Status
    1. User navigates to Profile → Recovery.
    2. Recovery score ring displays today's score with color (green/yellow/red).
    3. Score breakdown shows factor contributions (sleep, training load, HRV, readiness).
    4. Muscle group recovery map shows which muscles are ready vs. recovering.
    5. AI recommendation card: "Your recovery score is 72. A moderate intensity workout is recommended today."
    6. Recovery trend chart shows scores over the past 14 days.
    7. If score is below 40: "Rest Day Recommended" banner with active recovery suggestions.

  6.2 Subjective Readiness Check-In
    1. Optional morning notification: "How are you feeling today?"
    2. Quick survey: energy level (1-5), muscle soreness (1-5), motivation (1-5), stress level (1-5).
    3. Takes 15 seconds to complete.
    4. Results feed into recovery score calculation.

=====================================
7. TECHNICAL DESIGN
=====================================

  7.1 Recovery Score Algorithm

    Score = weighted sum of factors (0-100):

    Sleep Factor (35% weight):
      - Sleep quality score from the sleep tracking system.
      - Directly uses the sleep score (0-100).

    Training Load Factor (30% weight):
      - Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR).
      - Acute load: sum of workout volume × RPE over the last 7 days.
      - Chronic load: average weekly load over the last 28 days.
      - ACWR = Acute / Chronic.
      - Optimal range: 0.8-1.3 → 100 points.
      - 1.3-1.5 → 70 points.
      - 0.5-0.8 or 1.5-2.0 → 40 points.
      - <0.5 or >2.0 → 20 points (detraining or high injury risk).
      - If no workout data: 80 points (neutral).

    Heart Rate Factor (20% weight):
      - Resting heart rate compared to user's baseline (7-day average).
      - HR within 5% of baseline → 100 points.
      - HR 5-10% above baseline → 70 points.
      - HR >10% above baseline → 40 points (possible overtraining or illness).
      - If no HR data: 70 points (neutral).

    Subjective Readiness Factor (15% weight):
      - Average of energy, soreness (inverted), motivation, stress (inverted) ratings.
      - Mapped to 0-100 scale.
      - If no readiness data: 70 points (neutral).

    Final Score = (Sleep × 0.35) + (Training × 0.30) + (HR × 0.20) + (Readiness × 0.15)

  7.2 Muscle Group Recovery Tracking

    After each workout, mark trained muscle groups with the workout date and intensity.
    Recovery estimation by intensity:
      - Light intensity: 24-48 hours recovery.
      - Moderate intensity: 48-72 hours recovery.
      - High intensity: 72-96 hours recovery.
      - Very high intensity: 96-120 hours recovery.

    Status Categories:
      - Recovered: time since last training > estimated recovery time.
      - Recovering: time since last training is 50-100% of estimated recovery time.
      - Fatigued: time since last training is <50% of estimated recovery time.

    Display as a body map or grid showing muscle group status with color coding (green, yellow, red).

  7.3 Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio

    Weekly Training Load = Σ (session_duration_minutes × session_RPE) for all sessions in the week.
    Acute Load = sum of training load for the last 7 days.
    Chronic Load = average weekly training load over the last 28 days (rolling).
    ACWR = Acute Load / Chronic Load.

    Interpretation:
      - ACWR 0.8-1.3: "Sweet spot" — optimal training zone.
      - ACWR < 0.8: Detraining — training too little relative to capacity.
      - ACWR > 1.5: Danger zone — significantly higher than usual, injury risk elevated.

11. API REQUIREMENTS
=====================================

  GET /api/v1/recovery/score
    Query: date (optional, defaults to today)
    Response: { data: { score, factors: { sleep, trainingLoad, heartRate, readiness }, muscleStatus: [...] } }

  GET /api/v1/recovery/history
    Query: startDate, endDate
    Response: { data: RecoveryScore[] }

  POST /api/v1/recovery/readiness
    Request: { energy, soreness, motivation, stress, date }
    Response: { data: ReadinessSurvey }

  GET /api/v1/recovery/training-load
    Query: period (28 days default)
    Response: { data: { acuteLoad, chronicLoad, acwr, weeklyLoads: [...] } }

=====================================
13. AI REQUIREMENTS
=====================================

  - AI Coach uses recovery score to modulate workout recommendations.
  - Low recovery: AI suggests lighter workouts, rest, or active recovery.
  - High recovery: AI may suggest more challenging workouts.
  - AI considers muscle group recovery status when generating workouts (avoid fatigued muscles).
  - AI monitors ACWR trends and warns about injury risk when ratio exceeds 1.5.
  - AI function calculate_recovery returns the current recovery score with breakdown.

=====================================
14. ERROR HANDLING
=====================================

  - Missing data for a factor: use neutral default (70) and indicate "estimated" in the UI.
  - No workout data for ACWR calculation: skip training load factor, redistribute weight.
  - Heart rate data unavailable: skip HR factor, redistribute weight.
  - All factors missing: display "Insufficient data" instead of a misleading score.

=====================================
15. EDGE CASES
=====================================

  - User who only does cardio: adjust muscle recovery tracking for cardiovascular system.
  - User takes a week off: ACWR drops below 0.5 (detraining warning).
  - Very high training volume for advanced athletes: ensure ACWR baseline is calibrated to their level.
  - User completes readiness survey multiple times in a day: use most recent entry.
  - First-time user with no historical data: display introductory message, accumulate data before showing ACWR.
